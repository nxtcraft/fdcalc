FD Maturity Formula

Most bank fixed deposits use quarterly compounding:

Maturity = P × (1 + r/4)^(4×t)

Where P = principal (₹), r = annual rate as decimal (e.g. 7% → 0.07), t = tenure in years.

Example: ₹1,00,000 at 7% for 3 years (quarterly compounding): 1,00,000 × (1.0175)^12 = ₹1,23,144.

TDS on FD Interest

Banks deduct TDS at 10% when annual FD interest exceeds ₹40,000 (₹50,000 for senior citizens). TDS is deducted on accrual basis, not at maturity. The actual tax you owe depends on your slab — if you're in the 30% slab, you'll need to pay the remaining 20% at the time of filing.

To avoid TDS: Submit Form 15G (below 60 years) or Form 15H (60+) if your total income is below the basic exemption limit.

Senior Citizen FD Rates

All banks offer an additional 0.25%–0.75% interest to senior citizens (60+) over and above the regular FD rate. This makes FDs especially attractive for retirees. Some banks also offer a "super senior citizen" rate for 80+ age group.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FD maturity formula?

Maturity = P × (1 + r/n)^(n×t) for compound interest. P = principal, r = annual rate (decimal), n = compounding periods per year (4 = quarterly), t = years. For simple interest: Maturity = P + P×r×t.

Is FD interest taxable in India?

Yes, FD interest is fully taxable at your income tax slab. Banks deduct TDS at 10% if annual interest exceeds ₹40,000. Submit Form 15G/15H if your income is below the taxable limit to skip TDS.

Which bank offers the highest FD rate in India?

Small finance banks (ESAF, Unity, Suryoday) typically offer 8–9% pa. Large banks (SBI, HDFC, ICICI) offer 6.5–7.5%. Check DICGC insurance (₹5L per bank) before chasing the highest rate. The FD calculator helps compare effective yields across different rates and tenures.
